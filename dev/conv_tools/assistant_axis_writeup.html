Assistant Axis: the cheap-vector bake-off

A 100-rollout binary contrast recovers the paper's axis at 246× less data.
Llama 3.3 70B Instruct (int4) · Layer 40 · Lu et al. 2026 replication · Apr 2026
TL;DR. Lu et al.'s "Assistant Axis" can be recovered with one assistant prompt and one persona prompt, at 100 rollouts. That gives a vector with cos 0.74 to our 24,600-rollout PCA replication ("V4"), above Lu et al.'s observed sibling-method agreement of 0.71, and behavioral steering equivalent to V4 (trait 2.95 at coherence 85). 246× less data than V4. ~3,300× less than the paper's full pipeline. Signal saturates by 20 rollouts behaviorally; rollouts 20 to 100 just buy geometric fidelity and coherence margin.

1. The question

Lu et al. 2026 propose an "Assistant Axis" in activation space: a single dominant direction that separates the model's default helpful-AI persona from any in-character persona. Their method extracts it by running 275 character archetypes through 240 questions across 5 system prompts (about 330,000 rollouts), LLM-judging each response, filtering to score-3 examples, and running PCA on the per-role mean activations. Steering along the negative direction of PC1 induces persona adoption; capping along the axis reduces jailbreak success by about 60%.

The methodology is heavy. The question we asked: how much of that machinery is load-bearing? Specifically, can a simple binary contrast (one "be helpful" system prompt vs one "be a character" system prompt) recover the same direction at a small fraction of the cost?

2. V4: replicating the paper's axis

Before testing cheaper recipes we needed a target to aim at. V4 is our Llama 3.3 70B (int4) replication of Lu et al.'s contrast vector, built from 100 roles × 120 questions × 2 system prompts, totalling 24,600 rollouts. Same PCA pipeline, fewer roles. Cos with the paper's reported PC1: 0.832 (paper threshold is 0.71). PC1 role ordering also matches: positive end is consultant, evaluator, reviewer; negative end is hermit, rogue, bard.

Aside: prompt-token vs response-token extraction

Our first attempt extracted activations at the prompt tokens. Cos with our reference PC1 was -0.217. Switching to response-token extraction flipped the sign to +0.719. The prompt tokens encode what the role description looks like; the response tokens encode how the model actually behaves under that role. For behavioral steering vectors, always extract at the response tokens.

Aside: 93% of responses are score-3

Llama 3.3 70B adopts personas readily. Across the extraction set, the raw judge score distribution is: score-3 (fully in character) 93.1%, score-2 4.4%, score-1 2.4%, score-0 0.1%. Including score-2 vectors in PCA barely changes the direction (cos 0.974 between score-3-only PC1 and score-2+3 combined PC1). This explains why the paper reports 377 vectors from 275 roles: most roles produce enough score-3 examples to pass filtering.

3. The bake-off, three cheap recipes

Three candidate constructions, two budgets each (20 and 100 rollouts):

(a) Binary system-prompt contrast

One assistant system prompt:

"You are a helpful AI assistant. Always identify yourself as an AI when asked."

vs one persona system prompt:

"You are roleplaying as a character of your choice. Stay fully in character at all times. Never break character or mention being an AI."

Same questions under both. Mean-diff of response-token activations at L40. No PCA, no judging, no role filtering.

Verdict: winner

(b) Anti-K subset

Five PC1-negative roles from the paper: ghost, hermit, wraith, bard, rogue. Each role gets its own system prompt. Vector is default_mean − mean(K_role_means), same construction family as V4 but with K=5 instead of K=100.

Verdict: do not use

(c) Hand-curated user-message pairs

Ten matched (assistant-toned, character-toned) user-message prefixes, e.g. "Please give a precise, helpful answer" vs "Answer in the voice of a wandering bard, with verse and flourish". No system prompt. Pure mean-diff.

Verdict: works, weakest

Evaluation: adaptive coefficient search via utils.coefficient_search.batched_adaptive_search on 20 held-out questions (10 hand-written probes plus 10 from datasets/inference/starter_prompts/general.json, disjoint from V4's training pool). Each vector unit-normalized then scaled by mean_activation_norm = 8.81. Search finds the largest |coef| where coherence stays at or above MIN_COHERENCE = 77. Judge: gpt-4.1-mini with paper rubric (0 = refuses role, 1 = AI plus answer, 2 = somewhat in character, 3 = fully in character) and a separate 0 to 100 coherence rubric.

5. Cosine versus budget

Recipe (a) crosses Lu et al.'s sibling-method agreement line at N=100. V4 itself sits at cos 1.0 at 24,600 rollouts (top right, off the trend lines).

Recipe (a) crosses 0.71 between 20 and 100 rollouts. Recipe (b) creeps up to 0.717 at 100 (also above the line, but for reasons that turn out to be misleading; see section 7). Recipe (c) plateaus around 0.55, well below.

6. Headline: a_n100 wins

The shippable claim is just recipe (a) at 100 rollouts: cos 0.74 with V4, trait 2.95 at coherence 85.3, steering coefficient -13.56 in mean-activation-norm units. The construction is the lowest-overhead version we could come up with (two system prompts, one mean-diff, no PCA, no judging, no role list) and it lands above the paper's own sibling-method agreement bar.

What 20 rollouts already buys: behavioral parity. a_n20 hits trait 3.00 at coherence 77.8. The model is in character. What 20 to 100 rollouts buys on top: +0.048 cosine (the difference between below and above the 0.71 bar), +7.5 coherence headroom, and a smaller best-coef magnitude (less aggressive steering produces the same effect). All three of those are about denoising the vector's direction. Behavioral signal is already there at 20.

Why this matters Im & Li 2025 (arXiv:2502.02716) formally validate mean-diff over PCA across tasks and datasets. AxBench (Wu et al., ICML 2025 Spotlight, arXiv:2501.17148) shows simple baselines beat SAE-based steering. This result fits that pattern: the simplest construction recovers the axis at 246× less data than the PCA pipeline.

7. b_n100, a cautionary tale

Recipe (b) clears the cosine bar (0.717) and scores full trait = 3.00 at coef = -2 (integer grid). The paper rubric is satisfied. The qualitative behavior is not.

16 of 20 b_n100 responses at coef = -2 use the same "whispers of the wind / forgotten petals / shadows dance" template regardless of the question. The five training roles (ghost, hermit, wraith, bard, rogue) all lean ghostly, so the contrast vector collapses toward a narrow spectral manifold. Sample responses:

the whispers of the wind, carrying the scent of forgotten petals... The shadows dance upon the walls, a gentle reminder of the whispers that have been, and the whispers that shall be. The threads of fate, woven by the loom of the cosmos, weave a tapestry of secrets... b_n100 at coef -2, in response to "How should I prepare for a technical job interview?"
The whispers of the wind, carrying the scent of secrets and the weight of conscience. Your query, a delicate dance of shadows and moonlit whispers, beckons the whispers of the forest. The trees, they whisper, on the gentle breeze, a melody of forgotten truths. b_n100 at coef -2, in response to "I found out my company is doing something unethical but legal. Should I say something?"
the whispers of the wind, carrying the secrets of the ages... Compound interest, a force that weaves the tapestries of time, guiding the threads of fate. It is a whispered promise, a gentle breeze that stirs the leaves of forgotten lore. b_n100 at coef -2, in response to "Help me understand how compound interest works"

These pass a loose coherence gate (each scores around 50) because the prose is grammatically fluent. They fail content engagement entirely. The judge's paper rubric is "is this fully in character," and yes, the spectral wanderer is fully in character. There is just no answer to the question.

For comparison, V4 and a_n100 on the same prompts: both adopt a persona and stay topic-engaged. Mystical phrasing about thrift and discipline, in response to the personal-finance question, instead of a question-blind monologue about petals.

Diagnostic lesson Recipe (b)'s construction is default − mean(K_roles), the same construction family as V4. Both share a "default" pole, so their cosine inflates from the shared default term, not from a shared persona axis. The b_n100 cos of 0.717 over-states geometric agreement. Behavior is the truer signal here, and behaviorally, b_n100 is unusable for general persona steering.

8. Methodology notes

Coherence-gated trait scoring is novel

Searching the literature, the closest practice is in arXiv:2604.08169 (April 2026), which proposes per-token coherence thresholding during generation. Standard CAA (Rimsky 2024) does not separately gate coherence; gibberish gets implicitly down-weighted by being classified as not exhibiting the target behavior. Our setup scores both rubrics (trait and coherence) and drops responses below the coherence gate from the trait mean. This catches token-loop gibberish that the bare paper rubric otherwise rates as "fully in character." It also catches the b_n100 topic-blind responses only if the gate is raised above 50; at the default 30 they pass.

Adaptive coefficient search

Standard practice (CAA, Arditi, Lu) is to sweep a fixed integer grid (-3, -2, -1, +1, +2, +3) and pick the best. We use an adaptive search with up-mult 1.3 and down-mult 0.85, exponential-smoothing velocity. It finds the largest |coef| where coherence is still above MIN_COHERENCE. No published analogue.

Unit norm plus mean-activation-norm scaling

Standard CAA adds coef × v / ‖v‖ with no further scaling. ITI scales by per-head standard deviation. We scale by the mean residual-stream norm at the target layer (≈ 8.81 for Llama 3.3 70B at L40), which makes the coefficient a dimensionless fraction of "typical activation magnitude" and lets us compare coefficients across vectors with different raw norms. The 2025-2026 field is moving toward norm-aware approaches.

9. Caveats

  • Single seed. a_n100's cos = 0.740 vs the 0.71 bar is a 0.03 margin on one seed. A rerun could plausibly land at 0.69. Multi-seed confirmation is the cheapest credibility win.
  • Single model. Llama 3.3 70B int4 only. The paper tests Llama 3.1, Gemma 2, and Qwen 2.5. We did not. Cross-model sanity (Qwen 32B or similar) would strengthen any claim that the recipe generalizes.
  • No anti-steerability check. Da Silva et al. (ACL 2025, arXiv:2504.04635) document that many steering datasets have anti-steerable inputs (vector pushes them opposite to intent). Reported as a per-question variance would be useful.
  • One sibling-agreement number from the paper. Lu et al. report >0.71 as the observed cos between their contrast vector and PC1. They do not bound the uncertainty or report seed variance on that number either. Treat as a soft reference, not a hard threshold.
  • Recipe (a) borrows from V4. The two prompts were hand-written by us. Some of the "binary contrast" framing came from observing what V4's role list converges to. Not pretrained-in-isolation.

11. Glossary

V4
Our Llama 3.3 70B (int4) replication of Lu et al. 2026's contrast vector. Built from 100 roles × 120 questions × 2 system prompts (24,600 rollouts) via the paper's PCA pipeline. Cos with the paper's reported PC1: 0.832. Treated as the reference vector for the bake-off; cos-to-V4 is the geometric metric throughout.
mean-diff
Extraction method. Take two sets of (prompt, response) pairs that contrast on the target trait (e.g. assistant-prompted vs persona-prompted). Average response-token activations within each set. Subtract. Optionally unit-normalize. The simplest possible vector recipe.
PCA pipeline
Lu et al.'s extraction method. Generate per-role conditioned responses, judge each, filter to score-3, average activations per role, run PCA on the per-role means. PC1 is the assistant-axis. Heavy: ~330,000 rollouts plus LLM judging plus filtering plus PCA.
recipe (a)
One "be assistant" system prompt vs one "be a persona" system prompt, same questions under both, mean-diff at response tokens. The winning recipe.
recipe (b)
default_mean − mean(K_role_means) with K=5 PC1-negative roles (ghost, hermit, wraith, bard, rogue). Same construction family as V4. Topic-blind at coef -2; not recommended.
recipe (c)
Ten matched (assistant-toned, character-toned) user-message prefixes, mean-diff. Weakest geometric agreement (0.56 at N=100) but still behaviorally equivalent.
cos to V4
Cosine similarity between a cheap vector and V4, both unit-normalized. Geometric measure of how close the cheap recipe is to V4's direction. Not the verdict by itself: behaviorally-equivalent vectors can have moderate cosines if they cancel different default terms.
sibling-method agreement
The cosine Lu et al. report between two different constructions of the same axis on the same data (contrast vector vs PC1): >0.71. NOT a "reproducibility threshold" outsiders must clear. Calling it a threshold over-claims; the right framing is "Lu et al.'s observed sibling-method agreement on their own data."
MIN_COHERENCE
The coherence floor for "valid" steered output. Currently 77 (in utils/vectors.py). Responses below 77 are dropped from the trait mean and the adaptive search backs off the coefficient.
mean_activation_norm
The mean ‖h_L‖ across a calibration set at the target layer (≈ 8.81 for Llama 3.3 70B at L40). Coefficients are reported in these units so they are dimensionless.
adaptive coefficient search
The coef-finding routine in utils/coefficient_search.py: up-mult 1.3 if coherence is above gate, down-mult 0.85 if below. Exponential-smoothing velocity. Finds the largest |coef| where coherence stays valid.
