1. TL;DR

Across 9,545 steering runs covering 180 emotion traits on Qwen2.5-14B-Instruct, the median coherence collapses across a narrow band of perturbation ratio: 88% of runs are coherent (≥70) at α in [0.6, 0.8], 61% at [0.8, 1.0], 39% at [1.0, 1.2]. Linear interpolation of the per-bin median places the crossing at α ≈ 1.01. The cliff replicates a prior refusal-only observation on Gemma-2-2B (different model, different trait family), suggesting the perturbation-ratio framing is invariant across model and trait rather than refusal-specific.

Practical implication. Pick steering coefficients from α, not from raw c values. Set α = 0.7 to be safely in the high-coherence band, or push to 0.9 if you want maximum trait gain with a ~25% incoherence risk.

2. The claim, in one formula

For a unit-norm trait vector v applied with coefficient c at layer i:

αi  =  |c|
‖ hi ‖

‖hi‖ is the mean residual stream norm at layer i, estimated on a held-out prompt corpus. α = 1 means the steering perturbation has the magnitude of a typical activation at that layer; α > 1 pushes the residual outside its normal operating envelope.

Because both mean_diff and probe extraction methods unit-normalize their output, the ‖v‖ term drops out of the formula. This is a load-bearing simplification (see Limitations).

1.00–1.20 1,334 65.3 52.6 38.8

Interpolating between the (0.80–1.00) and (1.00–1.20) bins: median coherence crosses 70 at α ≈ 0.93. Interpolating across the full sweep instead (using bin midpoints 0.5/0.7/0.9/1.1 and medians 86/83/76/65) places the crossing at α ≈ 1.01. Both are consistent with the "cliff at ~1.0" framing; the sliver-between-bins ambiguity is real.

21% of runs have negative Δ. The trait vector points one way; the coefficient_search algorithm sometimes settles on coefficients that steer against the trait. The |Δ| column treats those as equivalent perturbations, which is the scaling-law framing but loses the sign distinction. If you specifically want "steering with the trait" behavior, filter to Δ > 0 first — the cliff location doesn't shift much, but mean Δ is higher.

7. Pipeline state

What runs end-to-end today

python analysis/steering/scaling_law.py \
    --steering-experiment emotion_set

Reads three inputs:

  • experiments/emotion_set/steering/.../results.jsonl — per-(trait, layer, coef) judge scores
  • experiments/mats-emergent-misalignment/analysis/activation_norms_14b.json — per-layer mean L2 norm for Qwen2.5-14B
  • Vector .pt files (only loaded indirectly via norm; mean_diff/probe are unit-norm by construction so the vector_norm term in the formula collapses to 1)

Writes five outputs into experiments/emotion_set/analysis/scaling_law/:

  • raw.jsonl — every (trait, layer, coef, α, coherence, Δ) row, 9,545 lines
  • binned.json — bin-level aggregates with provenance metadata
  • coherence_chart.json — dashboard-shape for the scatter + binned-median chart
  • delta_chart.json — same, for |Δ|
  • cliff_by_depth_chart.json — per-layer median + IQR band

Dashboard wiring

The finding at docs/viz_findings/coefficient-scaling-law.md consumes the three chart JSONs via :::chart scatter and :::chart line blocks. The charts read paths under /experiments/... directly (the static HTTP server in visualization/serve.py serves the whole repo root). No copy into docs/viz_findings/assets/ is needed for the live charts.

The render path required three extensions to the scatter renderer and one to the line renderer:

  • xaxis_type: 'log' / yaxis_type: 'log' — passes through to Plotly
  • binned_median_line: { x, y, name? } — overlay line trace on top of a scatter point cloud
  • floor_line: number | { y, label? } — horizontal dashed reference
  • (line renderer only) per-series fill + fillcolor for the IQR band, plus showlegend: false on the helper lower-bound trace

8. Limitations

Shipped in the finding (and worth flagging up front in any external writeup):

  1. One model. Qwen2.5-14B-Instruct only. Refusal on Gemma-2-2B is the only cross-model corroboration, and it's a different setup (single trait, different elicitation pipeline). Whether the same α threshold holds on Llama 3.x, Gemma 3, or Qwen 3 is open.
  2. Residual stream only. Not tested on attn_contribution, mlp_contribution, v_proj, k_proj. Each component has its own activation-norm scale, and the cliff location in α may shift.
  3. Unit-norm contrastive vectors only. Both mean_diff and probe unit-normalize at extraction, which is why the formula simplifies to |c| / ‖hi‖. SAE feature directions, raw mean_diff without normalization, multi-vector ensembles, or non-linear directions may not follow the same law.
  4. Single position. All vectors extracted at response[:5]. Other positions are untested.
  5. Activation norms borrowed. Norms come from a different experiment on the same model (mats-emergent-misalignment), not recomputed on the emotion-set prompt distribution. Assumed representative but not verified.
  6. Coefficient-search bias. Our search algorithm targets α near the cliff, so bins outside roughly [0.4, 1.2] are sparse. Tail behavior beyond α = 1.2 (n < 50 in our bins) was dropped from the headline tables.
  7. Coherence judge noise. Scoring uses gpt-4.1-mini; the llm-judge-optimization finding shows cross-judge absolute Spearman is only 0.11, so the 70 floor is judge-specific. The cliff shape should transfer (rank stability is higher than absolute agreement), but the exact threshold may not.
  8. 21% of runs steer against the trait (negative Δ). The |Δ| axis treats those as equivalent perturbations, which is the scaling-law framing but loses the sign distinction.
The biggest single concern: the activation-norm borrow. mats-emergent-misalignment's norm calibration used 90 prompts of general assistant conversation. emotion_set used contrastive emotion-elicitation prompts. If the steered-model activation magnitudes on emotion-elicitation prompts differ materially from the calibration distribution, α values are scaled by a constant that should be measured, not assumed. This is a one-evening fix: rerun analysis/vectors/compute_activation_norms.py on the emotion_set prompt set.

9. What to do next

Tier 1 — close the obvious holes (no GPU needed)

  • open Recompute activation norms on the emotion_set prompt distribution and re-derive α. Verify the cliff doesn't move.
  • open Add a "filter to Δ > 0" pass to confirm the cliff location holds when we restrict to "steering with the trait" runs.
  • open Compute trait-by-trait cliff locations and look at the outliers. Which 5 traits have cliff α < 0.7 (premature collapse)? Which 5 have α > 1.2 (resistant)? Hypotheses to test.

Tier 2 — cross-model corroboration (GPU, ~hours each)

  • open Rerun the same protocol on a different model. Llama 3.1 8B Instruct is the cheapest option; Qwen 3 14B would test "same family, different generation"; Gemma 3 27B would test "different family entirely." Pick one.
  • open Rerun on a different component. component-decomposition already has attn_contribution vectors for some traits; reuse those, recompute α using the attn norm, see if the cliff is in the same place.

Tier 3 — methodological extensions (research, not validation)

  • parked Does the law generalize to non-unit-norm vectors? Re-derive without the simplification, test on SAE decoder columns or raw mean_diff.
  • parked Multi-vector ensemble case: does α based on the L2 norm of the sum still predict the cliff?
  • parked Per-position cliff: extract at response[:10], response[:20], run the same analysis. Does the cliff move with position?
Open hypothesis: alignment with the natural activation manifold. At the same α some runs collapse and others don't, and 21% steer against the trait (negative Δ, suggesting the trait vector is anti-aligned with where the activation naturally points). A perturbation that lives in directions the model already uses should be less disruptive than one pushing into "unused" directions of the residual manifold.

Test design: for runs that break coherence at a given α, identify the generation token where output first becomes incoherent (first token with a sharp drop in log-prob, or first token after which the coherence judge scores the suffix below 70). Compute cos(v, ht) between the trait vector and the unsteered residual at that token. Compare against (a) tokens in non-collapsing runs at the same α, and (b) other tokens in the same run.

Prediction: collapse-tokens have lower cosine with v (more perpendicular to the manifold). If true, α alone is an incomplete predictor — the practical ceiling should be replaced by an alignment-aware threshold like α · (1 − |cos(v, ht)|) or similar. This would also explain the per-trait IQR of ±20–30% in cliff location: different traits have different average alignment with the residual manifold.

Run-level proxy already checked (null result): splitting our 9,545 rows by Δ sign within matched α bins shows no coherence difference — median coherence is within ±1 point between pos-Δ and neg-Δ runs in every bin (e.g. α [0.8, 1.0): 75.6 vs 75.5). So at run granularity the alignment hypothesis is wrong: anti-aligned trait vectors don't cause more incoherence at the same α. The per-token version of the test is still open and is the interesting one — the prediction would be that within a single collapsing generation, the specific token where coherence breaks has lower cosine with v than the surrounding tokens.
