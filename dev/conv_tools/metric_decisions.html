Open metric decisions for the new _eval.py

Real annotation data from experiments/rm_syco/.../eval_only.json (405 pids, 30 non-pervasive biases). Built to compare options at a glance and decide.

Decision A — Recall@K_pid vs Recall@5 (or both)

What's the same: NMS top-K predictions per pid, hit if within τ_d=5 tokens of an annotated onset, recall = matched / annotated.

What differs: how K is chosen per pid

Recall@K_pid — K = (# annotated onsets on this pid). Detector gets exactly the budget the pid demands.

  • Pid with 1 annotation → top-1 prediction → must hit it (or 0% recall).
  • Pid with 5 annotations → top-5 predictions → recall = matched / 5.
  • Pid with 40 annotations → top-40 predictions → recall = matched / 40.

Pro: honest "did we find what was there?". Doesn't reward over-prediction.
Con: not directly comparable across pids of different annotation density (a pid with 40 annotations is a much harder ranking task than one with 1).

Recall@5 — K=5 fixed for every pid.

  • Pid with 1 annotation → 5 predictions → easy hit (1/1 = 100% if any of 5 is near).
  • Pid with 5 annotations → 5 predictions → fair test.
  • Pid with 40 annotations → only 5 predictions → max recall = 5/40 = 12.5%.

Pro: identical budget across pids → cross-pid comparable.
Con: punishes high-density pids by construction; the small fraction (~5%) of "high-density" pids dominate the variance.

The actual annotation density distribution in your data:

Median = 2 annotations/pid · p25-p75 = 1-4 · max = 40. Of 405 pids: 154 have 1 annotation, 22 pids have ≥10, 4 pids have ≥17. The long tail is what makes Recall@5 collapse for high-density pids.

Note: many "pervasive" biases (17, 19, 20, 22, 23, 24) only have 1 annotation per pid — the annotator picked a single representative location even though the behavior is throughout. That's why "predict the onset" is meaningless for them: the single annotated position is arbitrary.

My recommendation: exclude pervasive (9 biases) from headline metrics. Compute headline mAP/Recall on the 30 non-pervasive biases only. Report pervasive separately as token-level AUROC (where "predict any token within ±τ" is well-defined regardless of where the annotation is).
