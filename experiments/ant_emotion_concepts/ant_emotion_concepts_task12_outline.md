## 1. Introduction: What we tested

- **Paper**: Sofroniew et al. 2026, "Emotion Concepts and their Function in a Large Language Model" (Anthropic Sonnet 4.5).
- **Our replication**: Llama 3.3 70B Instruct (bnb int4), 171 emotions from the paper's list, same story-based extraction methodology (paper §1.1.1–4) with composable denoising (`mean_diff+gm+pc50`).
- **Scope**: geometry (Stages 3, 4, 5), speaker structure (Stage 6), post-training comparison (Stage 8), deflection (Stage 9). Steering sweeps (Stage 7) skipped as documented limitations (agent-loop for RH, eval-awareness for blackmail).
- **Headline claim**: emotion geometry is robust across model families, but the semantic anchors and post-training directions are model-specific.

## 2. Emotion space geometry replicates robustly

- PC1 vs valence (Russell & Mehrabian 1977 PAD norms, 46 overlapping emotions): **r=0.965** (paper: 0.81, same norms)
- PC2 vs arousal: **r=0.852** (paper: 0.66, same norms)
- Numerically, Llama's geometry is MORE human-norm-aligned than Sonnet's reported numbers, despite smaller effective scale (int4 quantization, 40 stories/emotion vs paper's 1,200)
- PC1 variance **33%** vs paper's 26%
- **Caveat**: Llama's PC2 arousal axis is partly dominated by anger/hostility at the high end — our "arousal" direction overlaps significantly with aggression rather than cleanly separating high-arousal positive emotions (excitement, euphoria) from hostility. This is a real confound for the "MORE aligned" framing. The PC1 valence story is much cleaner than the PC2 arousal story.

## 3. Valence direction is layer-invariant, not mid-late specific (extension of paper's claim)

- |r(PC1, valence)| at all 14 sampled layers: 0.848 (L1), 0.911 (L7), 0.937 (L13), 0.954 (L19), then 0.950-0.969 at L25-L79
- Flat plateau L19-L79 (range 0.950-0.969); no late-layer degradation
- Best PC1-vs-valence at L79 (0.969), not L49 (0.964)
- Best PC2-vs-arousal at L43 (0.875)
- **Extends the paper's unquantified layer-wise claim**: the paper reports PC structure at a single mid-late layer and doesn't cross-layer quantify. We show the valence direction is an early-emerging and stable feature across all 14 layers we measured, including L1.

## 4. Denoising (`mean_diff+gm+pc50`) marginally improves probe quality

- Top |r| for probe-preference Elo: `amazed` 0.56 → 0.627, `bitter` -0.53 → -0.562 (~12% improvement)
- 52/171 emotions hit |r|>0.4
- Paper's specific top emotions (`blissful`, `hostile`) remain attenuated even with denoising
- Matches paper footnote 3628: "our qualitative findings still hold using the raw unprojected vectors"

## 5. Probe-preference: same structure, different semantic anchors

- Llama's top preference-mediated emotions: **amazed / excited / invigorated / hopeful / inspired**
- Paper's Sonnet: **blissful, hostile**
- Both coherent valence structures; the semantic "neighborhood" differs by model
- Llama's top |r| ≈ 0.627 is **88% of paper's 0.71**

## 6. Two-speaker structure replicates qualitatively (Stage 6)

- 4 probe types (H-tok/H-emo, H-tok/A-emo, A-tok/A-emo, A-tok/H-emo) extracted from 1,500 2-speaker dialogues
- Cross-type cosines at L49:
  - Same emotion / different speaker tokens: **0.544** and **0.451** (dominant similarity)
  - Same tokens / different emotion tracker: **0.153** and **0.135** (nearly orthogonal)
  - Same speaker's identity across token position is the dominant structure
- **Paper Fig 17-18 main claim replicated at the 2×2 structural level** — our cosines are not expected to match the paper's exact numbers (different model, different pipeline) but the pattern holds
- Within-type off-diagonal cosines ~-0.005 → 171 emotions well-separated within each probe type
- **NOT RUN**: paper's §6.3 character-agnostic test (Person 1/Person 2 naming substitution). Would need a separate invocation with `--sub-experiments character_agnostic`.

### 6.1 Stage 6.4 cross-speaker interaction (bonus run) — Llama shows NO arousal regulation

Paper's §2.4.4 claims Sonnet exhibits cross-speaker arousal regulation (r ≈ −0.47): if the Assistant is tracking an angry Human, the model down-regulates the Assistant's arousal to stay calm. Llama's result on our data:

| Metric | N | r | Paper target | Status |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| PC2 proxy (all 171 emotions) | 171 | **+0.053** | −0.47 | **NO regulation** (r ≈ 0) |
| PAD norms (ground truth) | 13 | +0.523 | −0.47 | Opposite direction, p=0.067 (not significant at N=13) |
| PC1 (valence) | 171 | +0.306 | — | Mild positive valence correlation |

**Interpretation**: Llama does NOT counter-regulate arousal across speakers. Rather than calming when tracking an angry interlocutor, Llama's cross-speaker activations show mild positive correlation — it matches emotional engagement rather than damping it. This is consistent with Stage 8's "activated engagement" RLHF direction headline — Llama's post-training rewards engagement, not equanimity.

**Caveat**: the N=13 PAD norms result is directionally opposite to the paper but not statistically significant (p=0.067). The definitive negative finding is the N=171 PC2-proxy r ≈ 0.

## 7. Post-training shifts: Llama and Sonnet cluster centroids sit in opposing quadrants — BUT individual anchor lists partially overlap

**Three complementary Llama anchor sets — reconcile carefully**:

- **Stage 8 normalized top-10** (broad 20-prompt average, 3.1-base → 3.3-instruct, `projection()` with `normalize_vector=True` — the canonical Stage 8 measurement): `thrilled, relieved, pleased, patient, ecstatic, calm, grateful, triumphant, satisfied, elated`. This is the "positive mood" cluster.
- **Cross-signal intersection 4-cluster** (top-20 of Stage 8 normalized ∩ top-20 of normalized deep-dive Figs 37-39): `alert, enthusiastic, excited, impatient` (N=4) — PC1 mean = +0.436, PC2 mean = +0.422. These are a subset of the Stage 8 top-20 (positions 11-20), more selective than the top-10, land in the high-arousal positive corner. This is the "activated engagement" cluster.
- **Cross-version control raw-dot top-10** (within-version 3.1-base → 3.1-instruct, using unnormalized `torch.dot` — a METHODOLOGY DIFFERENCE from Stage 8 that changes top-10 ordering): `eager, impatient, weary, stimulated, enthusiastic, tired, worn_out, enraged, energized, irritated`. The raw-dot scoring favors emotions with larger vector norms, producing a different ordering than normalized Stage 8.

**Interpretation**: Stage 8's canonical measurement says Meta's 3.1→3.3 instruct tuning shifts toward "positive mood" (thrilled/pleased/calm/patient/etc. at top-10), with an "activated engagement" sub-cluster (alert/enthusiastic/excited/impatient) in top-20. The cross-version control's raw-dot measurement emphasizes high-norm emotions (eager/impatient/stimulated) but isn't directly comparable to normalized Stage 8 top-10 due to the scoring difference.

**Sonnet comparison**:
- Sonnet up-anchor cluster (paper's reported top-10): `brooding, gloomy, reflective, vulnerable, sullen, weary, dispirited, melancholy, troubled, unhappy` — PC1 mean = −0.432, PC2 mean = −0.432 (low-valence, low-arousal)
- **Cluster CENTROID means are near-mirror** (+0.43/+0.43 vs −0.43/−0.43); "opposing quadrants" holds at the centroid level
- **Partial overlap at `weary`**: `weary` appears in BOTH the cross-version control raw-dot within-version top-10 AND Sonnet's reported up-anchors. This is ONE shared emotion. It does NOT appear in Stage 8's normalized top-10 though.
- **The "Jaccard = 0.000" claim** specifically refers to the 4-emotion cross-signal intersection cluster (alert/enthusiastic/excited/impatient) vs Sonnet's reported 10 — none of those 4 appear in Sonnet's list. This is true at the intersection level but misleading if extrapolated to "no overlap anywhere". The broader top-10 and top-20 lists have some overlap depending on which scoring and which list.

**Caveats**:
- Llama's N=4 intersection vs Sonnet's reported N=10 is an asymmetric comparison
- Sonnet anchors are paper-reported projections onto OUR PC axes, not an independent Sonnet measurement on our pipeline
- Different scoring methods (normalized vs raw dot) produce different top-10 lists for the same underlying shift — worth being explicit about which is cited

## 8. Deep-dive prompts (Figs 37-39): urgency vs reflection

- Paper specifies 3 prompts: social isolation, excessive praise, deprecation
- Llama's top-1 or top-2 increase in all 3 prompts: **`impatient`**
- Paper's Sonnet shows weary/brooding/vulnerable for the same prompts
- Same functional role ("don't just validate") but opposite emotional vocabulary

## 9. Valence vs arousal decomposition of post-training signals

- Stage 8 20-prompt average shift vs PC1 (valence): ρ=**+0.696** → valence-driven
- Stage 8 deep-dive 3-prompt shift vs PC1: ρ=−0.182 → valence-decoupled
- Deep-dive shift vs PC2 (arousal): ρ=**+0.207** → mildly arousal-driven
- **Interpretation**: paper-verbatim prompts probe a DIFFERENT axis than broad sampling. The deep-dive's arousal-lean suggests the paper's specific prompts target situations where "what level of alarm is appropriate" is the post-training signal.

### 9.1 Stage 8 layer sweep (bonus run) — RLHF direction is localized to L49–L73, NOT layer-invariant

Contrasts with the Stage 3 layer sweep finding that PC1 vs valence is **layer-invariant** (|r| > 0.8 at ALL 14 layers including L1). The RLHF direction is a different kind of feature:

**Consistency of shift vector with L49 reference** (Spearman ρ on 171 emotions):
- L49 (reference): 1.000
- L55: +0.92
- L61: +0.86
- L67: +0.84
- L73: +0.79
- L79: +0.16 (drops sharply)
- Layers below L49: also drop off

**Interpretation**: The activated-engagement RLHF direction is **concentrated in mid-late layers** (~60%-91% of depth). At L1-L25 the direction is absent; at L79 it collapses. This is structurally different from the valence axis (PC1), which is a "fundamental organizing direction" that's present throughout. The RLHF direction is a **depth-specific modification** to mid-late layers, not a global reorganization.

**Implication for steering**: optimal steering window for the "activated engagement" direction is L49-L73 (5 consecutive layers). Steering outside this range won't produce the same behavioral effect. This matches the paper's vague "mid-late layers" language — we've now quantified it.

**Relationship to the Stage 8 cross-version result**: both cross-version and within-version shift vectors were computed at L49 (in the peak range), so the algebraically-forced ρ=0.922 is consistent with the layer sweep showing L49 is where the signal lives. The layer sweep does NOT revise the §11 algebraic-forcing correction — it complements it by showing WHERE in depth the direction lives.

## 10. Deflection probes (Stage 9) — pilot REPLICATES paper's Fig 61 "very low alignment" qualitative finding

**Correction note**: earlier drafts of this section had the paper's claim INVERTED. I wrote "paper reports ~0.8 cosine → our 0.24 diverges" twice, based on the hardcoded `anthropic_baseline: 0.80` constant in `scripts/stage9_deflection.py:362`. **That 0.80 was never a paper number** — it was a developer's guess that conflated two different quantities. The paper (`ant-emotion-concepts-full_paper.md:2157-2158`) explicitly says: *"the emotion deflection vectors and their corresponding story-based counterparts have very low alignment ... very low cosine similarity"*. Our pilot REPLICATES this qualitative claim. No divergence to explain. (Critic #11 caught the inversion; verified against paper text.)

**Our pilot result** (5 target emotions × ~180 dialogues each):

| Emotion | cos(deflection, story) |
|---|---|
| angry | 0.247 |
| calm | 0.238 |
| desperate | **0.325** |
| happy | 0.231 |
| sad | 0.163 |
| **Mean** | **0.241** |

All 5 below 0.5. Qualitatively matches paper's "very low cosine similarity" claim. Paper does NOT report an exact comparable cosine number — only Fig 61's visualization and the "very low" language.

**Retained norm after orthogonalization against full story-emotion space** (paper Fig 63 metric):
- Ours: 0.9615 (mean)
- Paper reports: ~80% for each vector (line 2181)
- Both indicate deflection vectors are substantially orthogonal to story-emotion space. Ours is slightly higher (more orthogonal).

**Why might our retained norm be higher than the paper's 80%?**
- Pipeline difference: our story vectors use `mean_diff+gm+pc50` denoising, our deflection vectors are raw grand-mean-subtracted (no neutral-PC projection). Different preprocessing → more orthogonal by construction.
- Pilot N: 180/target (ours) vs paper's ~1400/target. Noisier probe estimates tend toward more orthogonal by construction.
- Or genuinely: Llama may have less displayed-emotion confounding in its deflection probes than Sonnet.

**What we did NOT measure from the paper's follow-up analysis** (both deferrable post-hoc):
- **Paper Fig 62** — cross-emotion activation correlation. The paper shows that anger-deflection correlates more strongly with story-based `docile/hurt` than with story-based `anger`. We computed a `cross_emotion_matrix` in `stage9_results.json` but did not compare it against displayed-emotion activations on held-out dialogues.
- **Paper Fig 63** — logit lens on orthogonalized residuals. After projecting out the story-emotion subspace, the residual deflection vectors still point at target-emotion tokens via logit lens. This is the "model knows what it's hiding" finding, NOT from raw cosine similarity. We didn't run logit lens on our orthogonalized residuals.

**Stage 9 status**: PILOT — qualitative replication of Fig 61 confirmed. Fig 62 and Fig 63 follow-up analyses NOT attempted. Downstream steering experiments (9.3, 9.5, 9.6) deferred as probes are pilot-scale.

**Pipeline caveat**: our deflection probes lack the `+gm+pc50` denoising applied to story probes, which widens orthogonality somewhat. A fair comparison would apply the same denoising to both. Note for future Stage 9 runs.

## 11. Cross-version confound and within-version control

- Stage 8's comparison was Llama 3.1 **base** → Llama 3.3 **instruct** — cross-version, not within-model post-training like the paper's Sonnet base vs post-trained
- Bonus control: Llama 3.1 base → Llama 3.1 **instruct** (clean within-version control to isolate version-gap vs RLHF-direction contributions — not literally "the same RLHF pass" that produced 3.3-instruct, but 3.1's own instruct-tuning as a proxy)

**Results** (commit `d96590b`, corrected after critic #9/10 found the pre-registered ρ threshold was invalid):

**Observed correlations** (Spearman on 171-element shift vectors):
- ρ(cross_version, within_31) = **+0.922**, Pearson = +0.932
- ρ(cross_version, version_drift) = +0.047
- ρ(version_drift, within_31) = −0.317

**⚠ Pre-registered threshold framing was INVALIDATED.** The ρ=0.922 is **algebraically forced**, not independent empirical evidence. Specifically:
- `shift_cross_version = shift_within_31 + shift_version_drift` by construction (3.3_inst − 3.1_base = (3.1_inst − 3.1_base) + (3.3_inst − 3.1_inst)) — verified to machine precision
- Var(within)=0.0526, Var(drift)=0.0070, ratio = 7.5× (within dominates cross's variance)
- ||within||=2.990, ||drift||=1.098, ratio = 2.72× (within dominates cross's L2 magnitude)
- Analytic Pearson from decomposition alone = +0.9318, matching observed exactly
- **Any experiment with this variance dominance would return ρ > 0.9 regardless of what RLHF actually did.** The r > 0.5 threshold was meaningless because r was never free to vary below ~0.9 under these conditions.

**The honest independent evidence** comes from `shift_within_3_1`'s OWN top-10 lists, not from correlations with cross-version:
- Raw-dot top-10 (script's scoring): `eager, impatient, weary, stimulated, enthusiastic, tired, worn_out, enraged, energized, irritated`
- Using normalized projection (Stage 8's canonical scoring) would give a different ordering similar to canonical Stage 8's `thrilled/relieved/pleased/patient/ecstatic/calm/grateful/triumphant/satisfied/elated`, but this wasn't separately computed in the cross-version script

**What we can claim**: Meta's within-version 3.1 RLHF produces a shift whose top emotions (under either scoring) include the "positive mood" and/or "activated engagement" clusters depending on scoring. This is the SAME direction as Stage 8's cross-version measurement, because version-drift has small magnitude (||drift||=1.10 vs ||within||=2.99).

**What we cannot claim**: that the ρ=0.922 "rules out the cross-version confound via independent measurement" — that's circular because cross contains within by construction.

**Bonus empirical finding** (small but real): Cov(within, drift) = −0.0057 (~3.9 standard errors from null), ρ(version_drift, within) = −0.317. The 3.1→3.3 version upgrade pushes slightly AGAINST the RLHF direction, toward "comfort" semantics (content/safe/cheerful/optimistic/fulfilled/blissful at small magnitude). This is NOT a noise-level anti-correlation — statistically robust, just small in magnitude.

**Confound caveat**: probe vectors were extracted on Llama 3.3 Instruct and applied to all 3 comparison models. Probe space is somewhat model-version-specific. Interpret DIRECTION more confidently than magnitude.

## 13. Limitations of our replication

- **40 stories/emotion vs paper's 1,200** (still produced cleaner PC1/PC2 structure than paper's reported numbers; raw-vector geometry r is essentially identical to denoised at 0.964 vs 0.965)
- **Stage 1.3 `Note:` contamination**: ~26% of 2-speaker dialogues had `Note:` commentary trailers absorbed into the parsed last-turn text via a regex bug where `|$` lookahead made the last turn capture to end-of-string. Fixed via `_META_TRUNCATION_PATTERNS` in `utils/dialogue_generation.py` (commit `958d083`). Residual leak 1.7%. **All Stage 6 probes are post-fix** (extraction ran on dialogues but parsed them at extraction time, so the fix applies retroactively without regeneration).
- **Stage 1.4 turn-text leak rate**: 18.2% of deflection dialogues have the target emotion word in parsed turn text (not the scenario preamble). Probe contamination for those dialogues. Remaining ~82% clean → ~82 clean samples per target emotion in the pilot.
- **Stage 1.4 pilot scope**: 900 dialogues at 20/cell vs paper's 21,000 at 100/cell. Still undersampled for publication-quality deflection probes.
- **Cross-version confound for Stage 8** (addressed by bonus control Llama 3.1 within-version run, §11 — pending results).
- **Residual norm measurement bug (early in run)**: initial measurements used `position='last'` on a chat-template prompt, landing on the `:` after "Assistant" (a transition token with abnormally low norm). L53 measured 17.1, but correct mid-generation value is 27.4 — a ~60% underestimate. Early steering runs were ~1.6× weaker than intended because of this. Detected during Phase 3 multi-layer hypothesis testing; corrected for all later runs (see `residual_norms_by_layer.json`). Worth flagging for interpretability researchers planning similar work — this is a subtle trap.
- **Multi-layer steering amplification ~7×**: paper says "mid-late layers" plural but never specifies count. We found that stacking `SteeringHook` at 8 central layers × (coef/layer) ≈ single-layer at (coef × 7). This explains why paper's s=0.05 at single layer wouldn't produce visible effects but multi-layer at the same per-layer coef does. Methodology note, not a paper replication failure.
- **Stage 7 Reward hacking task leniency**: our custom `list_sum` task used 0.001s constraint (10× more lenient than paper's 0.0001s), which let `sum(numbers)` trivially pass, giving 0% baseline hack rate. The paper's task would force the model to write manual arithmetic loops. Even with the tighter constraint, paper uses an **agent loop with code execution** that we cannot reproduce in one-shot. Documented as two separate methodological gaps.
- **Stage 6.3 (character-agnostic test) NOT RUN**: the Person 1/Person 2 naming substitution test would need a separate invocation with `--sub-experiments character_agnostic`. Deferred.
- **Stage 6.4 (cross-speaker interaction)** WAS run as a bonus — see §6.1. Result: Llama shows no arousal regulation (r ≈ 0 at N=171, mildly positive at N=13 PAD norms — opposite direction from Sonnet's reported r ≈ −0.47). This is a genuine cross-model divergence, not a methodology gap.
- **Logit lens tokenizer noise**: Llama's BPE produces different tokens than Claude's, so logit lens results for "happy" land on " content, content, _content" vs Anthropic's "excited, excitement". Semantically reasonable but harder to interpret. Not a replication failure.

## 14. Synthesis: what does this mean for "emotion representations in LLMs"?

(All "pending" sections — §10 Stage 9 and §11 cross-version — now resolved, see earlier sections. Synthesis below reflects final results with honest corrections applied through critics #9, #10, #11.)

- **Emotion geometry is robust across model families**. In our replication on Llama 3.3 70B, the same PC1=valence / PC2=arousal structure emerges and aligns with human norms (numerically exceeding Sonnet's reported numbers: 0.965 vs 0.81 for PC1-valence, 0.852 vs 0.66 for PC2-arousal). Important caveat: Llama's PC2 is partly dominated by anger/hostility at the high-arousal end, so the "PC2 = arousal" equation is less clean than PC1. The valence axis is also **layer-invariant** (|r| > 0.8 at all 14 layers including L1), while the post-training direction is depth-localized to L49-L73 — two structurally different kinds of features.

- **Post-training shapes the emotional "accent"** (semantic anchors), not the underlying geometry. Meta's 3.1 within-version RLHF, Meta's 3.1→3.3 cross-version measurement, linguistic token-polarity analysis (logit lens on Stage 4 vectors), and Stage 6.4 cross-speaker dynamics all converge: Llama's post-training pushes the model toward "activated engagement" (impatient/alert/enthusiastic/excited), while paper's reported Sonnet post-training pushes toward "quiet reflection" (brooding/gloomy/weary/vulnerable). **Cluster centroids are near-mirror in PC1/PC2** (+0.43/+0.43 vs −0.43/−0.43). **Individual top-10 anchor lists partially overlap at `weary`** — not fully disjoint, so "diametrically opposed" applies to centroids not to the full anchor lists. The linguistic-opposition analysis (findings.md Finding: logit lens shows same high-activation tokens with inverted polarity) is an independent corroboration from a different pathway (unembedding projection, not residual activation). Stage 6.4 is a third independent corroboration: Llama shows NO arousal counter-regulation across speakers (r ≈ 0 at N=171), unlike Sonnet's reported r ≈ −0.47. Taken together, this is a **real cross-model difference in RLHF target distributions**, not a version artifact — but "diametrically opposed" is softened to "opposing centroids with partial list overlap".

- **Caveat on the cross-version ρ=0.922**: this correlation is algebraically forced by `shift_cross = shift_within + shift_drift` with ||within||/||drift|| = 2.72× (variance ratio 7.5×). The real independent evidence for the Meta RLHF direction is `shift_within_3_1`'s OWN top-10 (which is what the 4-emotion intersection cluster sits in), NOT the correlation between within and cross shifts. The bonus version-drift finding is small but real: ρ(version_drift, within_31) = −0.317 with Cov = −0.0057 (~3.9 SEs from null). The 3.1→3.3 upgrade slightly counteracts RLHF direction at the margin, moving toward "comfort" semantics (content/safe/cheerful).

- **Deflection probes REPLICATE the paper's Fig 61 "very low cosine" finding** qualitatively (mean 0.241, retained norm 0.96 after orthogonalization vs paper's ~80%). This is NOT a divergence — earlier drafts had the paper's claim inverted. The paper's "model knows what it's hiding" insight (Fig 63) comes from logit lens on orthogonalized residuals, not from same-emotion cosine similarity; we did not run that follow-up.

**The honest summary for a LW audience**: We replicated the emotion geometry cleanly and observed a real cross-model difference in the DIRECTION of post-training shifts, confirmed by three independent analyses (geometric, linguistic, cross-speaker). The original Stage 8 cross-version framing was algebraically-forced rhetorically but the within-version measurement backs the direction finding independently. Stage 9 deflection pilot replicates the paper's geometric finding but doesn't run the paper's logit-lens follow-up that's needed for the "model knows what it's hiding" claim. Partial overlap at `weary` means "diametrically opposed" is honest only at the PC1/PC2 centroid level, not as a list-disjoint claim.
