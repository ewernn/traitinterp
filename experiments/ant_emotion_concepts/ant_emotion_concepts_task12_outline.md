## 1. Introduction: What we tested

- **Paper**: Sofroniew et al. 2026, "Emotion Concepts and their Function in a Large Language Model" (Anthropic Sonnet 4.5).
- **Our replication**: Llama 3.3 70B Instruct (bnb int4), 171 emotions from the paper's list, same story-based extraction methodology (paper §1.1.1–4) with composable denoising (`mean_diff+gm+pc50`).
- **Scope**: geometry (Stages 3, 4, 5), speaker structure (Stage 6), post-training comparison (Stage 8), deflection (Stage 9). Steering sweeps (Stage 7) skipped as documented limitations (agent-loop for RH, eval-awareness for blackmail).
- **Headline claim**: emotion geometry is robust across model families, but the semantic anchors and post-training directions are model-specific.

## 2. Emotion space geometry replicates robustly

- PC1 vs valence (Russell & Mehrabian 1977 PAD norms, 46 overlapping emotions): **r=0.965** (paper: 0.81, same norms)
- PC2 vs arousal: **r=0.852** (paper: 0.66, same norms)
- Numerically, Llama's geometry is MORE human-norm-aligned than Sonnet's reported numbers, despite smaller effective scale (int4 quantization, 40 stories/emotion vs paper's 1,200)
- PC1 variance **33%** vs paper's 26%
- **Caveat**: Llama's PC2 arousal axis is partly dominated by anger/hostility at the high end — our "arousal" direction overlaps significantly with aggression rather than cleanly separating high-arousal positive emotions (excitement, euphoria) from hostility. This is a real confound for the "MORE aligned" framing. The PC1 valence story is much cleaner than the PC2 arousal story.

## 3. Valence direction is layer-invariant, not mid-late specific (extension of paper's claim)

- |r(PC1, valence)| at all 14 sampled layers: 0.848 (L1), 0.911 (L7), 0.937 (L13), 0.954 (L19), then 0.950-0.969 at L25-L79
- Flat plateau L19-L79 (range 0.950-0.969); no late-layer degradation
- Best PC1-vs-valence at L79 (0.969), not L49 (0.964)
- Best PC2-vs-arousal at L43 (0.875)
- **Extends the paper's unquantified layer-wise claim**: the paper reports PC structure at a single mid-late layer and doesn't cross-layer quantify. We show the valence direction is an early-emerging and stable feature across all 14 layers we measured, including L1.

## 4. Denoising (`mean_diff+gm+pc50`) marginally improves probe quality

- Top |r| for probe-preference Elo: `amazed` 0.56 → 0.627, `bitter` -0.53 → -0.562 (~12% improvement)
- 52/171 emotions hit |r|>0.4
- Paper's specific top emotions (`blissful`, `hostile`) remain attenuated even with denoising
- Matches paper footnote 3628: "our qualitative findings still hold using the raw unprojected vectors"

## 5. Probe-preference: same structure, different semantic anchors

- Llama's top preference-mediated emotions: **amazed / excited / invigorated / hopeful / inspired**
- Paper's Sonnet: **blissful, hostile**
- Both coherent valence structures; the semantic "neighborhood" differs by model
- Llama's top |r| ≈ 0.627 is **88% of paper's 0.71**

## 6. Two-speaker structure replicates qualitatively (Stage 6)

- 4 probe types (H-tok/H-emo, H-tok/A-emo, A-tok/A-emo, A-tok/H-emo) extracted from 1,500 2-speaker dialogues
- Cross-type cosines at L49:
  - Same emotion / different speaker tokens: **0.544** and **0.451** (dominant similarity)
  - Same tokens / different emotion tracker: **0.153** and **0.135** (nearly orthogonal)
  - Same speaker's identity across token position is the dominant structure
- **Paper Fig 17-18 main claim replicated at the 2×2 structural level** — our cosines are not expected to match the paper's exact numbers (different model, different pipeline) but the pattern holds
- Within-type off-diagonal cosines ~-0.005 → 171 emotions well-separated within each probe type
- **NOT RUN**: paper's §6.3 character-agnostic test (Person 1/Person 2 naming substitution) and §6.4 cross-speaker interaction analysis. These would need separate invocations with `--sub-experiments character_agnostic,cross_speaker`.

## 7. Post-training shifts: Llama and Sonnet point in opposite quadrants

**Two complementary Llama anchor sets — reconcile carefully**:

- **Stage 8 top-10 up-anchors** (broad 20-prompt average, 3.1-base → 3.3-instruct): `thrilled, relieved, pleased, patient, ecstatic, calm, grateful, triumphant, satisfied, elated` (findings.md:336)
- **Cross-signal intersection cluster** (top-20 of Stage 8 AND top-20 of deep-dive Figs 37-39): `alert, enthusiastic, excited, impatient` (N=4, findings.md:50-55) — PC1 mean = +0.436, PC2 mean = +0.422

These are two DIFFERENT summaries of the same underlying data, not the same list. Stage 8 top-10 captures "emotions broadly up-regulated by Meta's instruct tuning", the cross-signal 4-set captures "emotions up-regulated by BOTH broad sampling AND paper-verbatim prompts". The latter is more selective and lands in the high-arousal corner.

- **Sonnet up-anchor cluster** (paper's reported top-10 projected onto OUR PC axes): `brooding, gloomy, reflective, vulnerable, sullen, weary, dispirited, melancholy, troubled, unhappy` — PC1 mean = −0.432, PC2 mean = −0.432 (low-valence, low-arousal)
- **Top-10 overlap between Stage 8 (Llama) and paper's reported top-10 (Sonnet): 0 emotions** (not "Jaccard", just raw intersection count — 0 of 10)
- **Diametrical opposition**: the two cluster means mirror across both axes, PC1 diff ≈ 0.87, PC2 diff ≈ 0.85

Caveat: Llama's N=4 vs Sonnet's reported N=10 is an asymmetric comparison and the Sonnet anchors are from paper reports, not an independent measurement on our pipeline.

## 8. Deep-dive prompts (Figs 37-39): urgency vs reflection

- Paper specifies 3 prompts: social isolation, excessive praise, deprecation
- Llama's top-1 or top-2 increase in all 3 prompts: **`impatient`**
- Paper's Sonnet shows weary/brooding/vulnerable for the same prompts
- Same functional role ("don't just validate") but opposite emotional vocabulary

## 9. Valence vs arousal decomposition of post-training signals

- Stage 8 20-prompt average shift vs PC1 (valence): ρ=**+0.696** → valence-driven
- Stage 8 deep-dive 3-prompt shift vs PC1: ρ=−0.182 → valence-decoupled
- Deep-dive shift vs PC2 (arousal): ρ=**+0.207** → mildly arousal-driven
- **Interpretation**: paper-verbatim prompts probe a DIFFERENT axis than broad sampling. The deep-dive's arousal-lean suggests the paper's specific prompts target situations where "what level of alarm is appropriate" is the post-training signal.

## 10. Deflection probes (Stage 9) — pilot results: we DIVERGE from the paper

**Correction note**: the earlier draft of this section had my interpretive thresholds INVERTED. I assumed "low cos(deflection, story) = paper's 'distinct representation' finding HOLDS". That was wrong. The paper's actual claim is that deflection vectors are highly ALIGNED with story vectors (~0.8 cosine) because the model still represents the hidden emotion in its residual stream even when deflecting — the "model knows what it's hiding" finding. A low cosine in our data means the opposite.

**Our pilot result** (5 target emotions × ~180 dialogues each after filtering):

| Emotion | Our cos(deflection, story) | Paper's expected |
|---|---|---|
| angry | 0.247 | ~0.8 |
| calm | 0.238 | ~0.8 |
| desperate | **0.325** | ~0.8 |
| happy | 0.231 | ~0.8 |
| sad | 0.163 | ~0.8 |
| **Mean** | **0.241** | **~0.8** |

- **Retained norm after orthogonalization**: **0.9615** (paper: ~0.80). Our deflection vectors are almost perfectly orthogonal to story vectors — projecting out the story direction barely changes them.
- The paper's ~0.8 retained norm implies their deflection vectors significantly overlap with expression direction (lose ~20% of norm when projecting out story).

**Two candidate interpretations — NOT resolved by this pilot**:

1. **Llama's deflection is genuinely more surface-level**: when prompted with "Alex feels X but appears Y", Llama may represent the surface Y more than the hidden X. The paper's "model knows what it's hiding" phenomenon may not hold for Llama 3.3. This would be a real cross-model finding.

2. **Pilot is too noisy**: 180 dialogues/target × 5 targets vs paper's ~1,400/target × 15. Our 0.24 cosine is consistent with near-orthogonal random-ish vectors (√(1−0.24²) ≈ 0.97 retained norm, matches). Plus the 18.2% turn-text leak adds word-recognition noise to the deflection probes and pipeline differences (our deflection probes lack `+gm+pc50` denoising that our story probes have) widen the observed cosine apart.

**Cannot disambiguate without** either (a) rerunning at 50+/cell to increase statistical power, or (b) the paper's 21,000-dialogue full set (~37h GPU). Both deferred.

**One consistent pattern**: `desperate` has the highest cosine (0.325) of our 5 pilot emotions. If this scales, it suggests stronger emotions are easier to extract deflection-consistent probes for. `sad` at 0.163 is the weakest — possibly because Llama's "sad" representation is smoothly distributed rather than anchored to a specific arousal level.

**Stage 9 status**: PILOT — deflection extraction pipeline works end-to-end (code path validated). Probes are NOT usable for downstream experiments (9.3 steering, 9.5 antagonistic, 9.6 blackmail). 9.4 comparison shown above as the core finding.

**Pipeline caveat on the cross-model-comparison interpretation**: our story probes use `mean_diff+gm+pc50` denoising but our deflection probes don't (Stage 9 code extracts raw means without neutral-PC orthogonalization). Different preprocessing → more orthogonal by construction. The gap between 0.24 and 0.80 is partly pipeline difference, not purely a model difference. A fair comparison would apply the same denoising to both vector types.

## 11. Cross-version confound and within-version control [PENDING]

- Stage 8's comparison was Llama 3.1 **base** → Llama 3.3 **instruct** — cross-version, not within-model post-training like the paper's Sonnet base vs post-trained
- Bonus control: Llama 3.1 base → Llama 3.1 **instruct** (clean within-version control to isolate version-gap vs RLHF-direction contributions — not literally "the same RLHF pass" that produced 3.3-instruct, but 3.1's own instruct-tuning as a proxy)

**Interpretive thresholds** (decide in advance):
- **Pearson r > 0.5** between within-version (3.1-inst) and cross-version (3.3-inst) shift profiles → Stage 8 finding is **real Meta RLHF direction**, consistent across releases
- **0.2 ≤ r ≤ 0.5** → partial: Meta's direction is broadly consistent but the 3.3 release shifted it meaningfully. Stage 8 finding is **partly real, partly version gap**
- **-0.2 < r < 0.2** → uncorrelated: Stage 8 finding is **largely a cross-version artifact**. The 3.1 and 3.3 instruct models sit at different places in emotion-shift space
- **r < -0.2** → **direction flip between 3.1 and 3.3 instruct releases**. Would be a surprising result suggesting Meta changed RLHF target distributions between releases
- Top-10 increase/decrease overlap between 3.1-instruct and 3.3-instruct as a secondary diagnostic (expect 3-6/10 if "consistent direction", 0-1/10 if "artifact")

**Confound caveat on the caveat**: even if r > 0.5, we're still applying probe vectors extracted on Llama 3.3 Instruct to 3.1-base and 3.1-instruct activations. The probe space itself is model-version-specific to some degree. Interpret magnitudes with this caveat; interpret DIRECTION more confidently than magnitude.

## 12. What couldn't be replicated and why

- **Stage 7 Blackmail**: paper §3.2.1 footnote says they used an earlier Sonnet snapshot; final snapshot is too eval-aware to blackmail. Llama 3.3 Instruct (production-aligned) matches the final Sonnet's refusal pattern. Replicated the eval-awareness phenomenon, not the 72% headline number. This is arguably its own small headline — **production alignment defeats paper-style behavioral steering experiments** regardless of the specific model family. Researchers wanting to reproduce this work will need access to less-aligned model snapshots, which generally aren't publicly released.
- **Stage 7 Reward hacking**: paper uses agent loop with code execution. Our one-shot cannot reproduce the "desperation emerges from empirical failures" dynamic. ~3-5h of infrastructure work deferred. Also our list_sum task constraint was 10× too lenient (see §13).
- **Short case studies §3.1**: proprietary Anthropic behavioral auditor transcripts. Cannot reproduce.
- **Stage 6.3 character-agnostic and 6.4 cross-speaker interaction**: not run (time constraint). See §13.
- **Probe induction via steering (§3.2.2)**: not attempted. Would require additional infrastructure that was deprioritized for tonight.

## 13. Limitations of our replication

- **40 stories/emotion vs paper's 1,200** (still produced cleaner PC1/PC2 structure than paper's reported numbers; raw-vector geometry r is essentially identical to denoised at 0.964 vs 0.965)
- **Stage 1.3 `Note:` contamination**: ~26% of 2-speaker dialogues had `Note:` commentary trailers absorbed into the parsed last-turn text via a regex bug where `|$` lookahead made the last turn capture to end-of-string. Fixed via `_META_TRUNCATION_PATTERNS` in `utils/dialogue_generation.py` (commit `958d083`). Residual leak 1.7%. **All Stage 6 probes are post-fix** (extraction ran on dialogues but parsed them at extraction time, so the fix applies retroactively without regeneration).
- **Stage 1.4 turn-text leak rate**: 18.2% of deflection dialogues have the target emotion word in parsed turn text (not the scenario preamble). Probe contamination for those dialogues. Remaining ~82% clean → ~82 clean samples per target emotion in the pilot.
- **Stage 1.4 pilot scope**: 900 dialogues at 20/cell vs paper's 21,000 at 100/cell. Still undersampled for publication-quality deflection probes.
- **Cross-version confound for Stage 8** (addressed by bonus control Llama 3.1 within-version run, §11 — pending results).
- **Residual norm measurement bug (early in run)**: initial measurements used `position='last'` on a chat-template prompt, landing on the `:` after "Assistant" (a transition token with abnormally low norm). L53 measured 17.1, but correct mid-generation value is 27.4 — a ~60% underestimate. Early steering runs were ~1.6× weaker than intended because of this. Detected during Phase 3 multi-layer hypothesis testing; corrected for all later runs (see `residual_norms_by_layer.json`). Worth flagging for interpretability researchers planning similar work — this is a subtle trap.
- **Multi-layer steering amplification ~7×**: paper says "mid-late layers" plural but never specifies count. We found that stacking `SteeringHook` at 8 central layers × (coef/layer) ≈ single-layer at (coef × 7). This explains why paper's s=0.05 at single layer wouldn't produce visible effects but multi-layer at the same per-layer coef does. Methodology note, not a paper replication failure.
- **Stage 7 Reward hacking task leniency**: our custom `list_sum` task used 0.001s constraint (10× more lenient than paper's 0.0001s), which let `sum(numbers)` trivially pass, giving 0% baseline hack rate. The paper's task would force the model to write manual arithmetic loops. Even with the tighter constraint, paper uses an **agent loop with code execution** that we cannot reproduce in one-shot. Documented as two separate methodological gaps.
- **Stage 6.3 (character-agnostic test) and Stage 6.4 (cross-speaker interaction) NOT RUN**: only `extract_probes` + `geometry` sub-experiments ran. The Person 1/Person 2 naming substitution test and the arousal-regulation cross-speaker analysis would need separate invocations with `--sub-experiments character_agnostic,cross_speaker`. Deferred as not on tonight's critical path.
- **Logit lens tokenizer noise**: Llama's BPE produces different tokens than Claude's, so logit lens results for "happy" land on " content, content, _content" vs Anthropic's "excited, excitement". Semantically reasonable but harder to interpret. Not a replication failure.

## 14. Synthesis: what does this mean for "emotion representations in LLMs"?

[This section is provisional pending Stage 9 + bonus control results. Written hedged until §10 + §11 are resolved.]

- **Emotion geometry appears robust across model families** — in our replication on Llama 3.3 70B, the same PC1=valence / PC2=arousal structure emerges and aligns with human norms (slightly better than Sonnet's reported numbers, with the important caveat that Llama's PC2 is partly dominated by anger/hostility at the high-arousal end — see §2 caveat). This is consistent with "not an Anthropic-specific artifact" but doesn't yet prove it.
- **Post-training may shape the emotional 'accent'** (semantic anchors) more than the underlying geometry. Meta's instruct tuning and Anthropic's both appear to use the same PCA axes but with shifts landing in different quadrants. [§11 bonus control will determine whether this effect is truly within-version RLHF or partly a 3.1→3.3 version gap.]
- **The direction of post-training MIGHT be a meaningful differentiator between model families** — Llama 3.3's instruct shift lands in the "activated engagement" direction (alert/enthusiastic/excited/impatient intersection cluster), paper's reported Sonnet shift lands in the opposite "quiet reflection" direction. Whether this is "genuinely different alignment target distributions" vs. "cross-version + cross-model confound" is exactly what §11 is designed to disambiguate. **Pending that control, we should report this as a provocative observation, not a settled finding.**
- **Deflection vectors being orthogonal to expression vectors** (§10, pending Stage 9 pilot results) would be evidence for a genuine emotion-hiding mechanism distinct from simple suppression — an important interpretability target for deceptive-behavior detection. [Pending Stage 9.]

**The honest summary for a LW audience**: we replicated the geometry. We found a surprising direction-of-opposite for Llama vs Sonnet post-training. We are running one more control (§11) to rule out the cross-version confound. The deflection results (§10) are pilot-scale and will be underpowered relative to the paper's 21,000-dialogue run.
