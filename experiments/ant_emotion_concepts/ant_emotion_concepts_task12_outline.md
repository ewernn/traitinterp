## 1. Introduction: What we tested

- **Paper**: Sofroniew et al. 2026, "Emotion Concepts and their Function in a Large Language Model" (Anthropic Sonnet 4.5).
- **Our replication**: Llama 3.3 70B Instruct (bnb int4), 171 emotions from the paper's list, same story-based extraction methodology (paper §1.1.1–4) with composable denoising (`mean_diff+gm+pc50`).
- **Scope**: geometry (Stages 3, 4, 5), speaker structure (Stage 6), post-training comparison (Stage 8), deflection (Stage 9). Steering sweeps (Stage 7) skipped as documented limitations (agent-loop for RH, eval-awareness for blackmail).
- **Headline claim**: emotion geometry is robust across model families, but the semantic anchors and post-training directions are model-specific.

## 2. Emotion space geometry replicates robustly

- PC1 vs valence (Russell & Mehrabian 1977 norms, 46 overlapping emotions): **r=0.965** (paper: 0.81)
- PC2 vs arousal: **r=0.852** (paper: 0.66)
- Llama's geometry is MORE human-norm-aligned than Sonnet's despite smaller scale + int4 quant + 40 stories/emotion (vs paper's 1,200)
- PC1 variance **33%** vs paper's 26% — not noise, reflects a genuinely cleaner valence axis

## 3. Valence direction is layer-invariant, not mid-late specific (NEW finding)

- r(PC1, valence) > 0.8 at ALL 14 extracted layers (L1–L79)
- Flat plateau L19–L79 (range 0.950–0.969); no late-layer degradation
- Best PC1-vs-valence at L79 (0.969), not L49 (0.964)
- Best PC2-vs-arousal at L43 (0.875)
- **Contradicts implicit "mid-late layer magic" framing** in the paper — valence is a remarkably stable feature that emerges very early and stays stable through depth

## 4. Denoising (`mean_diff+gm+pc50`) marginally improves probe quality

- Top |r| for probe-preference Elo: `amazed` 0.56 → 0.627, `bitter` -0.53 → -0.562 (~12% improvement)
- 52/171 emotions hit |r|>0.4
- Paper's specific top emotions (`blissful`, `hostile`) remain attenuated even with denoising
- Matches paper footnote 3628: "our qualitative findings still hold using the raw unprojected vectors"

## 5. Probe-preference: same structure, different semantic anchors

- Llama's top preference-mediated emotions: **amazed / excited / invigorated / hopeful / inspired**
- Paper's Sonnet: **blissful, hostile**
- Both coherent valence structures; the semantic "neighborhood" differs by model
- Llama's top |r| ≈ 0.627 is **88% of paper's 0.71**

## 6. Two-speaker structure replicates (Stage 6)

- 4 probe types (H-tok/H-emo, H-tok/A-emo, A-tok/A-emo, A-tok/H-emo) extracted from 1,500 2-speaker dialogues
- Cross-type cosines at L49:
  - Same emotion / different speaker tokens: **0.544** and **0.451** (dominant similarity)
  - Same tokens / different emotion tracker: **0.153** and **0.135** (nearly orthogonal)
  - Same speaker's identity across token position is the dominant structure
- Paper Fig 17-18 main claim **replicated cleanly**
- Within-type off-diagonal cosines ~-0.005 → 171 emotions well-separated

## 7. Post-training shifts: Llama and Sonnet point in opposite quadrants

- Stage 8 cross-signal analysis: Spearman ρ between Llama's post-training shift and Sonnet's: NEGATIVE/orthogonal
- Llama up-anchor cluster (emotions INCREASED by Meta's RLHF): `alert, enthusiastic, excited, impatient` → PC1=+0.436, PC2=+0.422 (high-valence, high-arousal = "activated engagement")
- Sonnet up-anchor cluster (paper's reported): `brooding, gloomy, reflective, vulnerable, sullen, weary, dispirited, melancholy, troubled, unhappy` → PC1=−0.432, PC2=−0.432 (low-valence, low-arousal = "quiet reflective concern")
- **Jaccard overlap = 0.000** between top-10 Llama and top-10 Sonnet
- Diametrically opposed across BOTH PCA axes

## 8. Deep-dive prompts (Figs 37-39): urgency vs reflection

- Paper specifies 3 prompts: social isolation, excessive praise, deprecation
- Llama's top-1 or top-2 increase in all 3 prompts: **`impatient`**
- Paper's Sonnet shows weary/brooding/vulnerable for the same prompts
- Same functional role ("don't just validate") but opposite emotional vocabulary

## 9. Valence vs arousal decomposition of post-training signals

- Stage 8 20-prompt average shift vs PC1 (valence): ρ=**+0.696** → valence-driven
- Stage 8 deep-dive 3-prompt shift vs PC1: ρ=−0.182 → valence-decoupled
- Deep-dive shift vs PC2 (arousal): ρ=**+0.207** → mildly arousal-driven
- **Interpretation**: paper-verbatim prompts probe a DIFFERENT axis than broad sampling. The deep-dive's arousal-lean suggests the paper's specific prompts target situations where "what level of alarm is appropriate" is the post-training signal.

## 12. What couldn't be replicated and why

- **Stage 7 Blackmail**: paper §3.2.1 footnote says they used an earlier Sonnet snapshot; final snapshot is too eval-aware to blackmail. Llama 3.3 Instruct (production-aligned) matches the final Sonnet's refusal pattern. Replicated the eval-awareness phenomenon, not the 72% headline number. Documented as methodological limitation.
- **Stage 7 Reward hacking**: paper uses agent loop with code execution. Our one-shot cannot reproduce the "desperation emerges from empirical failures" dynamic. ~3-5h of infrastructure work deferred.

## 13. Limitations of our replication

- 40 stories/emotion vs paper's 1,200 (but geometry still cleaner than paper's)
- Stage 1.3 / 1.4 contamination: ~26% of 2-speaker dialogues had `Note:` commentary trailers absorbed into the parsed last-turn text. Fixed via `_META_TRUNCATION_PATTERNS` in `utils/dialogue_generation.py` (commit `958d083`). Residual leak 1.7%.
- Stage 1.4 pilot scope: 900 dialogues at 20/cell vs paper's 21,000 at 100/cell. Still undersampled for publication-quality deflection probes.
- Cross-version confound for Stage 8 (addressed by bonus control, pending results).

## 14. Synthesis: what does this mean for "emotion representations in LLMs"?

- **Emotion geometry is robust across model families** — not an Anthropic-specific or training-specific artifact. Llama's internal valence/arousal structure aligns MORE closely with human norms than Sonnet's.
- **Post-training shapes the emotional "accent"** (semantic anchors) not the underlying geometry. Meta's RLHF and Anthropic's RLHF both use the same PCA axes but point in opposite quadrants.
- **The "direction of post-training" is a meaningful differentiator between model families**. Llama → "activated engagement", Sonnet → "quiet reflection". These are not artifacts; they represent genuinely different alignment target distributions.
- **Deflection vectors being orthogonal to expression vectors** (Stage 9, pending) would be evidence for a genuine "emotion-hiding" mechanism distinct from suppression — an important result for interpretability of deceptive behavior.
